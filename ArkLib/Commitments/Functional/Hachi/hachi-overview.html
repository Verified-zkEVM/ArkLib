The Hachi evaluation opening, seam by seam

Hachi [NOZ26] is a lattice-based multilinear polynomial commitment over the power-of-two cyclotomic ring Z_q[X]/(X^{2^α}+1). The core of one opening is a single line of nine reductions — from the quadratic-form evaluation (QuadEval) to a short-opening multilinear-evaluation claim on the committed table, which the closing end-piece then checks against the revealed witness. This map places every protocol-bearing file on that line, marks the bridges and subfolders, and shows exactly what is proven, repaired, or in progress.

Proof status

proven Sorry-free and complete. Every link of the nine-row chain is axiom-clean — only propext / Classical.choice / Quot.sound — and so is their composite iteration, verified by lake exe axiomsweep.
repaired Sorry-free, but deliberately not the paper's claim: the printed statement is false or unprovable as written, and what is proved here is a corrected variant. The theorem is real — the divergence is what this pill flags, so it is not counted as proven. Row 6 (Lemma 10) is the only one: the uniform-vector extraction is refuted by exists_nonzero_vanishing_on_axis_cross and replaced by per-coordinate scalar rounds with a nested binary-evaluation-tree zero test, at the cost of a temporary liftShort assumption. Each such divergence is recorded in docs/kb/audits/.

gap A documented soundness gap: the paper's step is (apparently) not knowledge-sound as stated, so the pull-back sorry stands as the record — the §4.5/§3.2 partial-evaluation gap in Recursion/ZBatchBridge.lean. No link on the current line carries it: the adapter holding it is future work, below.
planned Future work beyond the current opening line: recursive iteration, future heads and tails, and their target skeletons. These are not counted as current-line WIP.
The composed opening

The reduction line — QuadEval → evaluation claim

Top to bottom is one opening of an Rq-committed multilinear polynomial, exactly as composed in Composition.lean (iteration). Each link reduces one relation to the next, and every relation is the ordinary protocol relation. Where extraction can fail cryptographically instead of producing a witness (QuadEval's Module-SIS break, the weak-binding collisions of Figures 4–6), the link carries an escape event on the transcript tree (EscapeCWSSPackage.esc): a predicate on (statement, tree) entering the certificate as a disjunct of its conclusion, so no extractor can fabricate it. The final link outputs a bare multilinear-evaluation claim mle[w̃](a) = y′ on the committed table.
