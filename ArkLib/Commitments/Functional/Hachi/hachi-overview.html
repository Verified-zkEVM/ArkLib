The Hachi evaluation opening, seam by seam

Hachi [NOZ26] is a lattice-based multilinear polynomial commitment over the power-of-two cyclotomic ring Z_q[X]/(X^{2^α}+1). The core of one opening is a single line of nine reductions — from the quadratic-form evaluation (QuadEval) to a bare multilinear-evaluation claim on the committed table — followed by three §4.5 adapters (rows 10–12) that hand that claim to the next iteration. This map places every protocol-bearing file on that line, marks the bridges and subfolders, and shows exactly what is proven, repaired, in progress — or blocked on an open soundness gap in the paper's own argument.

The composed opening

The reduction line — QuadEval → evaluation claim → next iteration

Top to bottom is one opening of an Rq-committed multilinear polynomial, exactly as composed in Composition.lean (openingChain). Each link reduces one relation to the next, and every relation is the ordinary protocol relation. Where extraction can fail cryptographically instead of producing a witness (QuadEval's Module-SIS break, the weak-binding collisions of Figures 4–6), the link carries an escape event on the transcript tree (EscapeCWSSPackage.esc): a predicate on (statement, tree) entering the certificate as a disjunct of its conclusion, so no extractor can fabricate it. Row 9 outputs a bare multilinear-evaluation claim mle[w̃](a) = y′ on the committed table; rows 10–12 turn that into the next iteration's QuadEval statement over the next ring Φ′, closing the recursion loop.

The open gap, explained

Why the recursion is on hold — the §4.5 partial-evaluation gap

Row 9 ends the proven line with a single claim on the committed table: mle[w̃](a) = y′, where a is the vector of random sumcheck challenges (in the extension field F ≅ F_{q^k}) and y′ is pinned by the transcript — everything the iteration proved funnels into that one claim. The nonrecursive opening simply reveals w̃ and checks it; that path is proven (Correctness.lean, Concrete.lean). Recursion (§4.5) instead replaces the reveal with a proof by a smaller instance, in three moves — and the middle move appears not to be knowledge-sound as the paper states it.

① Peel — the partial evaluations
§4.5 · Eq. 24 · row 10
wip
Split the point a = (a₀, a₁) with a₁ the last κ coordinates, k = 2^κ the extension degree. The claim factors as y′ = ∑ᵢ eq(i,a₁)·yᵢ over the partial evaluations yᵢ := ∑ⱼ w̃_{j‖i}·eq(j,a₀), which the prover sends (the verifier derives one). Residual claims: each yᵢ matches the committed table. As a reduction, this seam is sound with zero error.
② Pack — one claim instead of k
§4.5 · Eqs. 25–26 · row 11
gap
Merge the k sub-tables into one F-entried table ŵⱼ := ∑ᵢ w̃_{j‖i}·Z^{⟨i⟩} along the power basis of F/F_q, and replace the k residual claims by the single packed claim ∑ⱼ ŵⱼ·eq(j,a₀) = ∑ᵢ yᵢ·Z^{⟨i⟩}, whose right side is public. This one equation is all the next iteration ever enforces — and it is strictly weaker than the k claims it stands for.
③ Hand off — into the next ring
§4.5 · Eqs. 27–28 · row 12
wip
Convert the packed F-claim into the next iteration's R′q-quadratic statement through ψ and the Theorem-2 trace identity, re-entering at QuadEval over Φ′ — with no new commitment: t is re-read at ring dimension d′ through the ψ/Z-packing bijection. This seam is pinned exactly by the trace, no slack; its open items are norm-parameter design (an inverse-ψ bound, linked regimes), not the soundness of the step itself.

What the paper's step relies on

Below Eq. (26) the packing is read as an equivalence: proving the single packed claim should be the same as proving all k partial-evaluation claims.
The forward direction is a substitution — swap two finite sums — and holds for the honest prover, so completeness is untouched.
The reverse direction leans on a basis intuition: 1, Z, …, Z^{k−1} is a basis of F over F_q, so a vanishing combination should force every coefficient to vanish. That is true — and it is exactly why the packing is lossless for the table entries, which genuinely live in F_q.

Why extraction fails anyway

Write δᵢ := pᵢ − yᵢ for the defects against the true partials. The packed claim says exactly ∑ᵢ δᵢ·Z^{⟨i⟩} = 0.
But the defects are not in F_q: pᵢ involves eq(j,a₀) at a random extension-field point — the sumcheck must run over F for its own soundness — so δᵢ ∈ F. Over F this is one linear equation in k unknowns: a solution space of F_q-dimension (k−1)·k. Multiplying a defect by Z slides it from one slot into the next.
The one-line analogy: base-10 digits make a representation unique because each digit is < 10; sums of multiples of powers of 10 with arbitrary coefficients are not unique — 30 = 3·10 + 0 = 2·10 + 10. The packing quietly switches from the first situation (entries) to the second (evaluations).

What a cheating prover gains
knowledge soundness of the recursive opening
Commit to any short table, run rows 1–9 for a false statement, and absorb the entire mismatch inside the k partial evaluations. Everything proven upstream — weak binding, the corrected Lemma 10, Lemma 11, the exact range proof — funnels through this one seam, and the seam leaks: none of it transports to the recursive opening. The same pattern with monomial weights xⁱ in place of eq-weights is §3.2's optimized F_q path (Eq. 11, Fig. 2 row 2), so the gap is not recursion-specific.
What stays sound
rows 1–9 · completeness · §3.1
The nonrecursive opening (rows 1–9 plus the reveal — Correctness.lean, Concrete.lean) never forms the yᵢ: the table itself is revealed, so Lemma 11 applies literally. Completeness is untouched everywhere — the honest yᵢ are the true partials. The generic §3.1 transformation (Fig. 2 row 1) is unaffected: it introduces no prover-chosen intermediate values and pins the whole packed evaluation Y ∈ Rq coefficient-by-coefficient. The paper's benchmarks are first-round-only and never exercise the step.

Root cause & the two known repairs

The tension: the sumcheck needs its challenges in the big field F for soundness; the packing's basis argument needs the packed coefficients in the small field F_q. §4.5 needs both at once, on the same objects. The step is one sentence in the paper (“…is equivalent to proving (26)”) with no lemma behind it — the honest direction is true and the basis intuition is true for entries, so the failure only shows once the extraction statement is typed precisely. That is how the formalization surfaced it.
Repair A — a fresh batching challenge over the peeled index: sample ρ after the yᵢ arrive and prove the ρ-weighted combination instead of the fixed Z-weighted one; a nonzero defect vector then survives with probability ≈ k/|F| (Schwartz–Zippel). One extra round, machinery close to row 5's batching bridge.
Repair B — drop the optimization: use the generic §3.1 packing (Fig. 2 row 1), which never forms the yᵢ, at the cost of κ extra variables and a different extraction/norm story for rows 10–12.
Both deviate from the printed protocol, so the decision sits upstream of any Lean work: write the counterexample up as a short note for the [NOZ26] authors, adopt whichever repair they take, then unblock rows 10 and 12. Until then, row 11's pull-back sorry stands — deliberately — as the record of where the paper's argument stands.
