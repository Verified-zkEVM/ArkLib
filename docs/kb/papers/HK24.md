# HK24

## At A Glance

ArkLib uses this reference for polynomial chunk recombination and preservation of that identity
under coordinated masks. The formalization provides reusable algebraic components for protocols.

## What ArkLib Uses From This Paper

Section 4.2 of the February 2025 revision describes Lagrange decomposition of a quotient
polynomial over disjoint cosets, and masks that preserve its recombination. ArkLib formalizes
the underlying polynomial identities in
[`CosetChunks.lean`](../../../ArkLib/Data/Polynomial/CosetChunks.lean).

The Lean construction uses a nonzero shift `s`, positive chunk count `c` and chunk size `m`,
and a primitive root `w` of order `c * m`. Chunk `i < c` consists of the points
`s * w ^ (i + c * t)` for `t < m`. Its normalized vanishing polynomial is
`Z_i(X) = (X / (s * w ^ i)) ^ m - 1`.

## Main ArkLib Touchpoints

- [`CosetChunks.lean`](../../../ArkLib/Data/Polynomial/CosetChunks.lean) defines the chunk

## Statement Correspondence

- `sum_chunkL_mul_chunkInterp` specializes the paper's Lagrange decomposition: every polynomial
  of degree below `c * m` is recovered from its degree-below-`m` chunk interpolants and the chunk
  factors `L_i`.
- `chunkL_mul_chunkZ` factors `L_i * Z_i` into a scalar `chunkWeight` times the same product of all
  chunk vanishing polynomials. This is the normalization identity behind mask cancellation.
- `sum_chunkL_mul_masked` specializes the paper's masked recombination identity. The first
  `c - 1` masks are supplied freely, and the final mask is minus their weighted sum, divided by
  the final chunk's nonzero weight.
- `chunkWeight_eq_prod_inv_eval` identifies the executable field-arithmetic weight with the
  polynomial normalization used by the proofs.

## Scope and Generalizations

The paper allows arbitrary disjoint cosets of a subgroup; ArkLib currently handles the regular
partition of one cyclic coset. Its algebraic identities hold over arbitrary fields with the
stated root and shift assumptions. The vanishing polynomials are normalized, so their scalar
weights account for that normalization.

The cancellation theorem allows arbitrary input polynomials and masks. It does not impose the
degree or sampling conditions needed for the paper's privacy analysis.

## Remaining Protocol Obligations

This module defines no commitment scheme, verifier, sampler, or transcript distribution.
In particular, it does not prove zero-knowledge. Applying the construction in an executable
protocol still requires executable polynomial representations, degree bounds for the masks,
the prescribed randomness, and the corresponding query-distribution analysis.
