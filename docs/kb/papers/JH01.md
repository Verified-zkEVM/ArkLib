# JH01

## At A Glance

`JH01` is Justesen–Høholdt, *Bounds on list decoding of MDS codes*, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 47(4),
1604–1609 (2001), DOI `10.1109/18.923744`. Its abstract describes list-decoding limitations for MDS
and Reed-Solomon codes, but the exact article is closed access and has not been inspected locally.

## What ArkLib Uses From This Paper

Nothing yet. [ABF26] Theorem 3.14 attributes a high-rate Reed-Solomon lower bound to JH01
Theorem 2, but that secondary rendering is not treated as a substitute for the primary source.

## Main ArkLib Touchpoints

`rs_codimension_one_list_size` in
[Bounds/ReedSolomon.lean](../../../ArkLib/Data/CodingTheory/ListDecodability/Bounds/ReedSolomon.lean)
is related support only: it is an internal, proved codimension-one interpolation lemma and is not
attributed to JH01.

## Known Divergences From ArkLib

The old theorem `rs_lambda_high_rate` was not a verified representation of JH01. It fixed block
length `j+1` and dimension `j`, hence rate `j/(j+1)`, while ABF26 prints approximately
`(j−1)/(j+1)`. Its proof used neither the prime-power sequence nor the congruence hypothesis: it
constructed the `j+1` drop-one-coordinate interpolants for any sufficiently large field. The result
has therefore been generalised and renamed `rs_codimension_one_list_size`, with all JH01 identity
removed.

No claim is made here that ABF26's rate is a defect in JH01: deciding that requires the exact
primary theorem, which remains unavailable.

## Open Formalization Gaps

Obtain the exact article and verify Theorem 2's code convention, evaluation set, congruence
conditions, list radius and size, and asymptotic quantifiers. Then formalise it separately without
increasing the direct-admit budget. The internal codimension-one lemma does not close this row.
