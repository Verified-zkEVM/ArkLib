# JH01

## At A Glance

`JH01` is Justesen–Høholdt, *Bounds on list decoding of MDS codes*, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 47(4)
(2001) — list-size lower bounds for MDS codes in the *high-rate* regime, complementing the low-rate
bounds of [BKR06] and [GHSZ02].

## What ArkLib Uses From This Paper

Theorem 2, as rendered by [ABF26] Theorem 3.14: for infinitely many prime powers `q ≡ 1 (mod j+1)`
there is a code `RS[F_q, L, k]` with `|L| = j+1`, rate `≈ (j−1)/(j+1)`, and a word `w` with
`|Λ(C, 1/(j+1), w)| > j`.

## Known Divergences From ArkLib

**This paper is not in the local reference cache**, so nothing below is checked against the original.

**The printed rate is a paper defect.** At block length `j+1`, dimension `j` gives rate `j/(j+1)`, not
`(j−1)/(j+1)`; no degree convention reconciles them, since degree-`≤ (j−1)` *is* dimension `j`. The only
dimension yielding `(j−1)/(j+1)` is `j−1`, which is unsatisfiable: minimum distance `3` against a radius
admitting one error caps the list at `1`. At `j = 2` the discrepancy is `2/3` versus `1/3`.

**At the satisfiable dimension the conclusion is elementary and the source's conjuncts are inert.** With
`k = j`, the `j+1` drop-one-coordinate interpolants of any `w ∉ C` are distinct codewords within
distance `1`, giving `j+1 > j` for *every* `q ≥ j+2` — using neither `IsPrimePow` nor `q ≡ 1 (mod j+1)`.
So the Lean theorem does not capture whatever [JH01] proves at rate `(j−1)/(j+1)`; the modular condition is the
existence condition for `μ_{j+1} ⊆ F_q^×`, suggesting the source pins `L = μ_{j+1}` and concludes
something sharper.
